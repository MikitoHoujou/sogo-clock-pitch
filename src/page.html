The wall we are painting with light

会場と制約Dark marble, no speakers, a timer

Tap the markers. The clock is the main stage: the marble around it is dark and orange and soaks up light, so contrast drops the further we move from the clock.

Palette rule: white and blue read, green and orange vanish

Each swatch is light projected onto a strip of the orange-brown marble. The team's rule from the session is to lead with high-contrast whites and blues. Confirm with a night test on site.

  • SurfaceClock is the primary stage. White cloth or paper over the walls could lift contrast, but the SOGO mark must stay uncovered.
  • SoundNo output on site. Story, pacing and jokes have to work silently.
  • PlaybackAutomated timer run by Sogo's rigging and projection contractor. We need their composition template.
  • TestingNight test on site to check real contrast against the lobby lights.
  • BudgetNo cap given. We show three tiers per direction.
Seven directions, each in three tiers

提案の方向性Made by hand, played by the hour

A to C came out of the 18 Nov session. D to G are four more from creative direction, each aimed at a different word in the brief. All seven are built from real materials or real footage shot in camera, not CGI animation. That keeps cost per film low enough to make many short films instead of one expensive loop, and it reads as handmade to people tired of AI and CG.

Direction C, carried through the year

影絵の四季A shadow on the wall, the season in the clock

One format for every season. A shadow made by real hands, or a cut-paper puppet on a rod, travels along the lit band toward the clock. Inside the clock face it turns into the real thing in full colour. Only the shadow and the reveal change, so each new season is a short shoot, not a new production. Pick a month.

Many films, one wall

一日の上映A different three minutes every hour

We chose a multi-film day over a single repeating loop. If 14:00 is not the same as 11:00, people time their visit, come back, and post what they saw. Pick an hour; the lineup follows the direction selected above. Hours with a blue dot have a concept frame. Titles are a first draft.

What we are advising against

キャラクター案についてWhy we are not leading with characters

We looked hard at character animation. Twelve distinct three-minute character films would need specialist freelance animators and cost far more per minute than the directions above.

Advise against

A family's day

A household moving through morning, afternoon and evening. Relatable, but complex, expensive and less magnetic.

Advise against

One mascot

Fatigue sets in across seasons, and a client may adopt the mascot permanently, which limits every season after.

If mandated

A cast of 5–10 creatures

Unrelated personas rotating by hour, in the spirit of an ensemble kids' show. Simple sketch style, sub-minute bits, interacting with products.

Advise against

Rebuilding the old karakuri

Copying the original or "It's a Small World" mechanics carries copyright risk and invites comparison with the original.

Advise against

Physical puppets

¥100–120k per puppet, set building, and puppets lose contrast on dark marble. Flat 2D or cut paper does better.

Later phase

Live interaction

QR-code Tanabata wishes or a live camera feed need an engineering partner we do not have for spring.

If characters are required, we recommend one repeating hourly loop rather than a different film each hour. Direction G is the one character idea we would back: the building itself is the face, so there is no separate mascot to tire of.

Two story options

ストーリー案Two stories for the clock

Each option is one film that plays on the hour and changes with the season. Option 01 is a silent love story under the clock. Option 02 sends a seasonal greeting from the clock to everyone below.

Option 01

“Meet me under the clock”

時計の下で、待ち合わせ

Hourly · 2–3 min · a single narrative film

A fashion-conscious couple, seen only as silhouettes, meet under the clock in the face of seasonal adversity.

Clocks in department store lobbies have long been meeting points, and the clock at Sogo Yokohama is no different. This concept turns that everyday moment into the story. We never see the couple’s faces, only their silhouettes, fashion items and accessories. Because they are anonymous, anyone watching can see themselves in them, and we avoid designing a mascot. The season itself is the obstacle to their meeting, through wind, rain or crowds, and it also becomes the magic once they finally meet.

The story, autumn edition

  1. On the hour. A panel of the clock opens as a door. The silhouette of a well-dressed young woman steps out and closes the door behind her. Because she is a silhouette, the one or two fashion items we leave in colour pop out: an elegant coat, a seasonal scarf, a hat or bag, red lipstick.
  2. Waiting. She shuffles, looks around and paces from left to right inside the clock, glancing up at the hands as though waiting.
  3. The gust. A big autumn breeze blows her hair and coat, and colourful leaves sweep the full length of the lobby from the right. On cue, the silhouette of a man is blown into the scene in a ball. He stands, dusts himself off, shows his own autumn look, checks his watch and realises he is late.
  4. Against the weather. The wind changes. Straining against it, he presses on with renewed urgency, until the weather turns again: rain, lightning and a familiar, futile fight with his umbrella.
  5. Meanwhile. She has perched on the bottom edge of the clock, feet dangling, playfully unaware of what he is going through to reach her.
  6. Arrival. After two or three autumn weather adventures he collapses into the clock, startling her, then composes himself. Her reaction can be a laugh or pure relief; both work.
  7. Together. They embrace, the clock and the whole wall burst into a celebration of the season with autumn confetti, and they step into the clock together.

Close-ups between the wide shots add drama and impact. The scenes are illustrative and simple, with the approachable warmth of 2D.

Option 02

Seasonal Greetings

時候の挨拶

Handwriting · origami · seasonal flowers · weather

A handwritten letter appears in the clock, folds itself into a paper plane or an origami animal, and sets off on a journey through the seasons and landscapes of Japan to reach someone. When it arrives, it unfolds to reveal its seasonal greeting across the wall.

It is about sharing, and about caring for the customer.

The spring edition

The letter rides a spring breeze over fields and rivers, collecting petals along its folds, and opens to a message welcoming the new season.

A new letter for every edition

  • SummerA greeting carried through heat haze and wind chimes: 暑中お見舞い申し上げます.
  • AutumnA letter flying under the moon.
  • New YearA New Year card travelling through falling snow.

24 messages a year? One for each of the 24 solar terms (二十四節気) would give the wall a new greeting about every two weeks.

Nostalgia
The greeting-card ritual means a lot to families and older visitors.
Childhood

Warmth

Season
It gives Sogo a seasonal message to share with customers each time.
